Chhath Puja in Bangalore — The Bihari Diaspora's Most Sacred Festival

Chhath Mahaparva is among the most ancient and uncompromising Vedic sun-worship traditions in the world, originating in the Mithila region of Bihar and Eastern Uttar Pradesh. Unlike many other Hindu festivals that are celebrated with professional ritual assistance, Chhath has traditionally been a household vrat — a deep personal covenant with Surya Devata and Chhathi Maiya — observed with strict fasting, purity protocols, and precise seasonal timing.

For the large Bihari, Bhojpuri, Maithili, and Purvanchal community residing in Bangalore's technology corridors — in Whitefield, Electronic City, HSR Layout, Sarjapur Road, and Marathahalli — Chhath presents a unique challenge. Away from ancestral village ponds, family Ghats, and neighbourhood community support, Bangalore-based devotees must recreate the entire sacred environment — including Ghat arrangements, Soop and Dala materials, Thekua and Kheer preparation, and the precise Arghya timing at sunset and sunrise — entirely on their own.

Pandit Ji Express provides dedicated Purohit guidance to Bihari and North Indian families in Bangalore, ensuring Chhath Puja is observed with full Shastric authenticity, regional ritual fidelity, and practical logistics support for the Bangalore context.

The Four-Day Chhath Puja Schedule — Vidhi & Significance

Day-by-Day Chhath Mahaparva Vidhi:
  1. Day 1 — Nahay-Khay (Kartik Chaturthi): The vrat begins with a sacred bath in a river or clean water source. The Vrati (the fasting devotee) eats a single meal of Lauki (bottle gourd) sabzi with chana dal and rice cooked in an utterly pure (saatvik) environment. No non-vegetarian food, salt, or onion-garlic is consumed in the house from this day onward.
  2. Day 2 — Kharna (Kartik Panchami): Full-day fast is observed without water. In the evening, after sunset, the Vrati prepares rice Kheer (Kheer Poda) and Roti cooked on a mango-wood fire in an earthen pot. This Prasad is offered to Surya Devata and then distributed to family and neighbours. Our pandit can conduct the Kharna Prasad sanctification rituals at your home.
  3. Day 3 — Sandhya Arghya / Sanjhi Arghya (Kartik Shashthi): The most visually iconic day of Chhath — fasting Vratis carry Dala (bamboo baskets) filled with Soop, sugarcane, seasonal fruits, Thekua, and diyas to a Ghat (riverbank or lake). Standing waist-deep in water, they offer Arghya to the setting sun with Sanskrit mantras and Chhathi Maiya folk prayers, facing west toward the setting Surya. This continues until the sun fully sets.
  4. Day 4 — Usha Arghya / Bhorkai Arghya (Kartik Saptami): Before sunrise, Vratis return to the Ghat to offer the final Arghya to the rising sun, facing east. Prasad (Thekua, fruits, coconut) is distributed and the vrat is completed. The fast is broken with Prasad.

Chhath Puja Ghat Locations in Bangalore

Community Chhath Ghat celebrations in Bangalore are organized by Bihari, Purvanchal, and North Indian Samaj associations across the city. Common Ghat locations in recent years have included:

  • Hebbal Lake (Kempapura): One of the most well-organized Chhath community celebrations in North Bangalore, typically managed by local Bihari Samaj committees.
  • Ulsoor Lake (Central Bangalore): A central lake accessible from Indira Nagar, Frazer Town, and HAL areas with community Ghat arrangements.
  • Nagavara Lake (RT Nagar): North Bangalore Chhath location used by communities from Yelahanka and Hebbal corridors.
  • Sarakki Lake (JP Nagar): South Bangalore community Ghat popular with HSR Layout and Bannerghatta Road devotees.
  • Society Terrace or Pool Arrangements: Many apartment societies in Whitefield, Electronic City, and Sarjapur Road create temporary Ghat arrangements in their swimming pool areas or open terraces with large water containers. Our pandit can guide and supervise these arrangements.

Families should verify current Ghat location and management with their local Bihari/North Indian community association each year, as locations may shift based on lake-board permissions.

Complete Chhath Puja Samagri Checklist for Bangalore Families

Below is a traditional Chhath Puja samagri list verified against established Bihar regional practice:

Chhath Samagri List (Traditional Bihar Vidhi):
  • Soop (bamboo or brass winnowing fan)
  • Dala (large bamboo or brass basket)
  • Sugarcane stalks (Ganna) — 5 to 7
  • Thekua (wheat flour & jaggery sweet)
  • Rice Kheer (for Kharna Prasad)
  • Coconut (Nariyal) — whole
  • Banana bunches (Kela)
  • Orange, Lemon, Amla
  • Beel fruit, Suran (yam)
  • Earthen diyas & wicks
  • Pure cow ghee (Desi Ghee)
  • Mango leaves & Darba grass
  • New yellow/saffron cloth (dhoti-dupatta)
  • Gangajal or clean river water in copper pot

Frequently Asked Questions: Chhath Puja in Bangalore

When is Chhath Puja in 2026 in Bangalore?
Chhath Puja 2026 falls on Kartik Shukla Shashthi, typically in October or November. The four-day vrat begins on Chaturthi (Nahay-Khay), followed by Panchami (Kharna), Shashthi (Sandhya Arghya at sunset), and Saptami (Usha Arghya at sunrise).
