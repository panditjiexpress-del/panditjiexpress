Diwali is the single most important household puja of the year for North Indian families. Performed correctly with a qualified Purohit, the Lakshmi Puja becomes a transformative spiritual act — not merely a ritual formality.

Diwali Lakshmi Puja in Bangalore — The North Indian Tradition

For families from Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Delhi-NCR, and other North Indian states now residing in Bangalore, Diwali Lakshmi Puja is the centerpiece of the Diwali festival — a solemn, joyous invitation to Goddess Mahalakshmi to enter and bless the home with prosperity, abundance, and divine grace. The ceremony is observed on the night of Kartik Amavasya, when the moon is absent and the universe is in its most receptive state for Lakshmi's arrival.

The North Indian Diwali Puja tradition is distinct from South Indian and Gujarati Diwali customs in several ways — it follows the Shodashopachara (16-step) Mahalakshmi Puja, includes a dedicated Kubera Puja for material prosperity, and traditionally incorporates the Dhanpati (Lord Kubera as master of wealth) invocation alongside Goddess Lakshmi. Mantras are from the Shri Sukta and Lakshmi Ashtakam from the Vedic corpus.

For North Indian families in Bangalore's apartment towers and gated communities — from Whitefield and Electronic City to HSR Layout and Sarjapur Road — having a knowledgeable Hindi-speaking Purohit conduct the Diwali Lakshmi Puja ensures that the ceremony is performed with full Shastric depth, proper muhurat timing, and the spiritual authenticity that the occasion demands.

The North Indian Diwali Lakshmi Puja — Complete Vidhi

The traditional North Indian Diwali Lakshmi Puja follows the Shodashopachara method — a sixteen-step ceremonial offering that systematically honours the Goddess through every sensory and spiritual dimension:

Shodashopachara Diwali Lakshmi Puja Steps:
  1. Dhyana (Meditation & Invocation): The Purohit meditates on Mahalakshmi's form and invokes her divine presence into the murti or image placed on the altar.
  2. Avahana (Welcome): Formal invitation to the Goddess with specific Vedic mantras from the Shri Sukta to descend and be present at the altar.
  3. Asana (Sacred Seat): Offering of a symbolic seat to the Goddess, accompanied by appropriate mantras.
  4. Padya (Foot Wash): Symbolic washing of the Goddess's feet with water infused with flowers and Gangajal.
  5. Arghya (Honoring Offering): Offering of scented water in a copper vessel as an act of reverence.
  6. Achaman (Sipping Offering): Offering of pure water for the Goddess to sip.
  7. Madhuparka & Snan (Purification Bath): The murti is bathed in Panchamrit (milk, curd, honey, ghee, and sugar) and then in Gangajal, with accompanying Shri Sukta recitation.
  8. Vastra (Clothing): Offering of new red or golden cloth to the Goddess.
  9. Pushpa (Flowers): Offering of fresh lotus flowers, marigold garlands, and rose petals with the names of Mahalakshmi's 108 forms.
  10. Dhupa (Incense): Offering of fragrant incense or agarbatti to please the Goddess's sense of smell.
  11. Dipa (Sacred Lamp): Offering of the ghee lamp (Panch-batti or Divya-diya) — the most auspicious moment of the Diwali Puja.
  12. Naivedya (Food Offering): Offering of Kheel-Batasha, Kesar Peda, seasonal fruits, and coconut as sacred food.
  13. Tambulam (Betel): Offering of betel leaves and betel nuts to the Goddess.
  14. Dakshinam (Gift): Symbolic offering of gold coins, silver items, or monetary dakshina to the Goddess.
  15. Pradakshina (Circumambulation): Circling the altar clockwise three times as a mark of reverence.
  16. Aarti & Visarjan (Farewell): Concluding Lakshmi Aarti, distribution of Prasad, and gentle farewell prayer asking the Goddess to remain in the home throughout the year.

Kubera Puja — The North Indian Diwali Wealth Ritual

Uniquely central to the North Indian Diwali tradition is the Kubera Puja — a dedicated worship of Deva Kubera, the celestial treasurer and lord of wealth, performed alongside Mahalakshmi. This is especially significant for business-owning families, traders, and professionals.

During the Kubera Puja, our Purohit invokes Lord Kubera with the Kubera Dhyan mantra (Yakshaya Kuberaya Vaishravanaya Dhanmanyadhipataye...), offers special Prasad of dry fruits, coins, and sweetmeats, and performs the Dhanpati Japa for financial prosperity and protection of accumulated wealth. Account books (Bahi-Khata), business ledgers, and new accounting records are also placed on the altar and blessed during this ceremony.

Diwali Lakshmi Puja Timing — Muhurat in Bangalore

The Diwali Lakshmi Puja muhurat is calculated based on three astrological windows on Kartik Amavasya:

  • Pradosh Kaal: The twilight window approximately 1.5 hours after Bangalore sunset — this is the primary, most auspicious Lakshmi Puja muhurat. The exact window shifts by 10–15 minutes each year based on the sun's position.
  • Vrishabha Lagna: When the Vrishabha (Taurus) Lagna is rising on the eastern horizon during Pradosh Kaal, this is the ideal confluence for Lakshmi Puja, as Taurus is the sign of material prosperity and Mahalakshmi's domain.
  • Nishita Kaal (Midnight): For Tantric or Shakta traditions, the midnight Nishita Kaal is also used for Lakshmi Puja by certain regional communities.

Our pandit will calculate and communicate the precise muhurat window for Bangalore's geographical coordinates (13°N, 77.6°E) well in advance of the ceremony.

  • Betel leaves and betel nuts (Paan-Supari)
  • Gold/silver coins or silver items
  • New account book (Bahi-Khata) for Kubera Puja
  • Janeu, Mauli thread

Frequently Asked Questions: Diwali Lakshmi Puja in Bangalore

When is the Diwali Lakshmi Puja muhurat in 2026 in Bangalore?
Diwali 2026 falls on Kartik Amavasya in October or November. The traditional North Indian Lakshmi Puja muhurat is during Pradosh Kaal (approximately 1.5 hours after Bangalore sunset) coinciding with Vrishabha Lagna. Contact us for the precise 2026 Bangalore muhurat window well before Diwali.
How long does Diwali Lakshmi Puja take with a pandit?
A complete Shodashopachara Mahalakshmi Puja with Kubera Puja and Aarti takes 45 minutes to 1.5 hours depending on the ceremony depth, family size, and whether an extended Shri Sukta or Lakshmi Sahasranam recitation is included. For business establishments, add 20–30 minutes for Bahi-Khata Puja.
Do you provide Diwali Lakshmi Puja for offices and shops in Bangalore?
Yes. We conduct Diwali Udyog Lakshmi Puja, Bahi-Khata Puja, and Kubera Puja for offices, shops, restaurants, and business establishments across Bangalore. WhatsApp us with your location and the number of employees for a tailored arrangement.
Is Diwali Lakshmi Puja the same as Navratri Lakshmi Puja?
No. Navratri Lakshmi Puja (performed on Ashtami/Navami of Navratri) focuses on the warrior and maternal aspects of the Goddess. Diwali Lakshmi Puja specifically invokes Mahalakshmi as the Goddess of Abundance, Wealth, and Prosperity on Kartik Amavasya — a distinct occasion with different mantras, a different Shodashopachara method, and the unique addition of Kubera Puja.
